TrueMile EV — Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 24, 2026

TrueMile EV ("the app") is built and operated by Gate Engineering LLC. This policy describes exactly what data the app handles, where it goes, and how you can delete it. It is written to be read, not skimmed — there is nothing hidden in it.

The short version

  • Your data is used to run your own trip and charging history. Nothing else.
  • No ads. No sale of data. No third-party analytics or tracking SDKs.
  • Your data lives in the app's own private cloud database, keyed to your account.
  • Subscriptions are paid through Google Play; the app never sees your card or payment details.
  • Deleting your account in the app deletes your cloud data.

Where it lives

Your data is stored in the app's own database project (hosted on Supabase), in a private account scoped to your sign-in. It is not shared with, rented to, or sold to anyone. Access is restricted by row-level security to your own account.

Some data also lives only on your device: app settings, diagnostic logs (off by default, opt-in, and only ever leave the phone if you personally share the file), and cached map data.

Background location

The app asks for background ("Allow all the time") location because automatic trip logging is the core feature: it detects when you start driving and records the route without you opening the app. Location is recorded for your own trip history. It is not used for advertising, profiling, or location sharing of any kind. You can decline background location; trip logging then only works while the app is open.

Third-party services the app talks to

The app queries a small number of services to do its job. Each receives only what the feature needs, never your identity:

  • Charger directories (NREL / Open Charge Map): the map region you are viewing, to list nearby chargers.
  • Routing (OSRM): route coordinates, to draw and time your planned route and to align recorded trips to roads.
  • Map tiles (OpenFreeMap): the map area you are viewing, to draw the map itself.
  • Address search (Photon): the destination text you type, to suggest places.
  • Charger naming (OpenStreetMap / Overpass): the coordinates of a charger you arrive at, to label the session with the station's name.
  • Elevation & weather (Open-Elevation / Open-Meteo): route coordinates, to enrich trip details with grade and conditions.
  • VIN decoding (NHTSA, a US government service): the VIN you enter or scan, to fill in your vehicle's specifications.
  • Fuel prices (EIA, a US government service): your state, to compare your charging cost against local gas prices.
  • Area lookup (FCC, a US government service): the coordinates of saved charge sessions — including home charges — may be geocoded once to determine the US state they occurred in, for the gas-price comparison above.
  • Community charger prices (optional, on by default, can be turned off in Settings): when you log a paid public charge, the station's location, price, and speed can be contributed to a shared community dataset under a random anonymous identifier — never your email, account, or VIN. Home charging is never contributed.

None of these are analytics or advertising services.

Subscriptions and purchases

TrueMile EV offers Pro and Max subscriptions, sold through Google Play.

  • Google Play handles the payment. Your card or other payment method, billing address, and the charge itself are processed entirely by Google under Google's own privacy policy. The app never sees, receives, or stores card numbers or any other payment details.
  • What the app stores so it can unlock the plan you bought, in the same private database as the rest of your account: the Google Play purchase token, the order ID, the product and plan (for example, Pro billed annually), the subscription's state (active, in a grace period, canceled, expired, and so on), whether it began with a trial, whether it renews automatically, and its expiry or renewal date — and Google Play's own record of the subscription as Google returns it, which includes the country of purchase, the start date and, if you cancel, any reason you give Google. The app checks these with Google Play to confirm the subscription is genuine and current.
  • What the app sends to Google with a purchase: a one-way hash (SHA-256) of your account ID, passed as Google Play's "obfuscated account ID", which Google uses to detect fraud and abuse. It cannot be turned back into your account ID and never includes your email or name.
  • Status updates from Google: when a subscription renews, is canceled, refunded, or expires, Google Play notifies the app's server, which uses the notice only to keep your plan current.
  • Canceling: manage or cancel your subscription in the Google Play Store app → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions. Deleting your TrueMile account does not cancel a Google Play subscription — cancel it in Google Play first. The app's subscription records are deleted with your account; Google keeps its own purchase records under its own policy.

What the app does NOT do

  • No advertising, ad SDKs, or ad identifiers.
  • No sale or sharing of personal data with data brokers.
  • No third-party analytics (no Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, Mixpanel, etc.).
  • No reading of contacts, messages, photos (other than vehicle photos you take in-app), or any data unrelated to driving and charging.

Retention and deletion

  • Your cloud data is kept for as long as you keep your account.
  • Settings → Delete account permanently removes your personal cloud data — your identity, email, trips, routes, GPS locations, home coordinates, charging locations, prices and costs, notes, settings, and generated reports.
  • Deleting your account does not cancel a Google Play subscription; cancel it in Google Play.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all local data from the device.
  • Crash reports and anonymous community price contributions are retained only in aggregate and are not linked to a deleted account.

The vehicle's health record stays with the vehicle. When you delete your account, an anonymized battery and condition history is retained, keyed only to the vehicle's VIN — like a service book that follows the car to its next owner. It contains vehicle facts only:

  • The vehicle's identity card: make, model, year, battery pack size, and the door-jamb sticker specifications (manufacture date, weight ratings, tire and rim sizes, cold tire pressures).
  • Charging sessions at day-level dates: odometer, battery percentage before/after, energy added, duration, fast-vs-slow charging, outside temperature, and the charging-power curve.
  • Battery state-of-health readings by date.
  • Diagnostic trouble codes read from the vehicle, and tire fitment records.

It can never contain your name, email, or any account link; any location; any price, cost, or payment information; any trip or route; or any free-text notes — the retained record has no fields for them. If you also want this vehicle history removed, email us with the VIN before deleting your account and we will exclude it.

One code is kept so the introductory period can't be restarted. New accounts get an introductory period during which every feature is open. To keep that to one per vehicle, we store a one-way code derived from the vehicle's VIN and from the OBD adapter's address, together with a one-way code for the account that used it. The codes are irreversible: they contain no VIN, no adapter address and no account identifier, and nothing can be read back out of them — they are only ever compared for equality, so the single question they answer is "has this vehicle already had its introductory period, and was it this same account?" They are kept for up to 90 days after they are created, including past account deletion — that is the point of them — and are then deleted. They are never used for advertising, profiling or any other purpose.
